# NestUp — The First Commercial
## Complete production package · Creative Direction, Production, Edit

# PART ONE — THREE CONCEPTS

## Concept A · "Two Doors"
*(your story, refined)*

Two mothers in neighbouring buildings. Same morning, same need, four metres apart,
no idea. The app shows them the same playground meetup. They go. The children play.
They talk.

**Why it works:** perfectly legible. Anyone understands it in one viewing, including
a deputy mayor half-reading their phone. The premise is the product.

**Why I'm not recommending it:** it is a *demonstration* with actors in it. The story
exists to explain the mechanism, and an audience can feel that. Its emotional ceiling
is "that's clever." We're aiming higher.

**Logo test:** without the logo it's a nice scene. Not a film.

---

## Concept B · "The People You Almost Met"

One parent. One ordinary Tel Aviv day. As she moves through it — Rothschild, a café,
the playground, the market — we start noticing what she doesn't: another parent with
a pram crossing behind her, a dad two tables away doing exactly what she's doing, a
family leaving the playground as she arrives. Never framed as loss. Framed as
**abundance she can't see.** The city is thick with her people. She's walking through
them.

Then, softly, the reveal: she can see them.

**Why it works:** the film *is* the thesis. "The community already exists — NestUp
reveals it" isn't stated, it's the visual grammar of every shot. This is the Google
"Year in Search" engine: meaning through accumulation.

**Why it's not the pick:** it's a beautiful essay, and essays admire rather than move.
It stays slightly cerebral. No one's heart breaks.

**Logo test:** passes. Genuinely good film about how cities hide people from each other.

---

## Concept C · "They Were Here All Along" ★ RECOMMENDED

**Open at the end.**

Six seconds of a group that is unmistakably *close*. Late afternoon, a Tel Aviv
playground. Four or five parents, a scatter of toddlers, coffee cups on a wall, one
child asleep against a shoulder that isn't their parent's. Nobody performs. It's the
easy, unglamorous warmth of people who have done this many times.

Hard cut. The light snaps to cold morning.

**Eleven weeks earlier.** Now we play forward — and now the near-misses land, because
we know what these strangers are *going to be to each other*. The woman passing on
the far pavement isn't a stranger any more. We've met her. We saw her laughing six
seconds ago.

The app appears once. Three seconds.

Then the first meeting: two people who don't know they're at the beginning of
something. Slightly awkward. A real introduction. The children get there first.

Last frame: the card.

> ### הם היו כאן כל הזמן.

**Why this is the film:**

The other two concepts ask the audience to imagine a payoff. This one *hands them the
payoff in the first six seconds* and then shows the cost of it never happening. Every
near-miss in the middle section carries weight the same shot could never carry in a
forward-told story. You aren't watching two strangers — you're watching friends who
haven't met yet, and the ache of that is the entire product benefit.

It also solves the municipal problem cleanly. The opening image *is* the outcome the
city wants to buy: a functioning neighbourhood community. We show them the deliverable
in the first six seconds, then explain what stands in its way.

**Logo test:** passes hardest. Strip the branding and it's still a film about how
close we live to people we'd love. NestUp just gives that feeling a name.

**Structural note:** the middle section borrows Concept B's engine wholesale. B isn't
discarded — it's the second act.

## Character bible

**MAYA — 31.** Our lead. Curly dark hair, usually half-escaping a clip. Slightly
under-slept. Practical rather than styled: olive linen shirt, straight jeans, canvas
trainers scuffed at the toe, a tote with a muslin cloth hanging out of it. Wedding ring.
No makeup. Reads as competent and a little bored — a woman with a free morning and
nothing to point it at. **Never sad.**

**DANA — 28.** The other half of the introduction. Straight brown hair, grey tee, denim
jacket knotted at the waist. Marginally more put-together than Maya, which is the joke
of their friendship. Warmer face, quicker to laugh.

**AVIV — 19 months.** Maya's. Sunhat, striped romper, permanently sticky.
**NOA — 22 months.** Dana's. Yellow t-shirt, bare feet by the end.

**THE GROUP (shots 1–2):** two more parents, deliberately not matched — one man in his
40s, one woman in her 20s with a baby carrier. Community is not a demographic.

### Casting note that matters more than any prompt

**They must not be beautiful. They must be specific.** Under-eye shadow. A scrunchie on
a wrist. Yesterday's shirt. The single fastest way to make this read as AI is symmetrical
faces and good hair. Every generation that comes back glossy gets rejected on sight.

---

## Character consistency strategy

This is the highest-risk item in the entire production. Six shots contain Maya's face.
Without locking, you get six different women and the film is dead.

1. **Lock two reference stills first** — `ref_maya.png`, `ref_dana.png` — before a single
   frame of video. Front-on, neutral, even light, plain background.
2. **Seedance 2.0 for every shot containing a recognisable face**, passing those stills
   as `image_references`. It is the only model in the roster with genuine cross-shot
   identity holding.
3. **Veo 3 only where no face is legible** — shots 1 and 3 are wide enough that identity
   doesn't read. Veo's architectural light is the best available and worth using where
   it's safe.
4. **Wardrobe never changes** except the light. Continuity is cheaper than correction.
5. Generate all Maya shots **in one session, back to back**, so the reference conditioning
   stays consistent.

## Visual style guide

**Camera language.** Static or nearly so. One locked frame per moment. The near-misses
work *because* the camera doesn't chase them — the other parent enters and leaves the
frame while the camera stays with Maya, which is exactly what it feels like to miss
someone. The only movement in the film: a slow rack focus in shot 5, and a barely-there
handheld drift on the intimate shots. **No drone. No orbit. No push-in except none.**
Anything showy reads as tourism.

**Lenses.** 35mm for the wides, 50mm for the people. Shallow but not creamy — T2.8, not
T1.4. We want the city legible behind them; the neighbourhood is a character and can't
be blurred into bokeh soup.

**Locations.**
- Rothschild Boulevard central path — ficus canopy, cyclists, benches
- A residential Bauhaus street in Lev Ha'ir — cream render, rounded balconies, scooters
- A neighbourhood playground with rubber matting and a chain swing (not a landmark park)
- A side-street café with four outdoor tables — not a design café

**Not Yarkon Park at golden hour.** Not the beach. Not Neve Tzedek's photogenic alley.
The moment it looks like the Ministry of Tourism shot it, the film loses its claim to
being about real life. Ordinary streets, honestly framed, are more persuasive.

**Colour grade.** One grade across everything, screen recording included — the app must
live in the same world, not float above it.
- Act one (shots 1–2): warm, 5600K drifting amber, long low sun
- Act two (shots 3–6): pull 400K cooler, lift shadows toward blue-grey. Mediterranean
  shade genuinely is blue; honouring it stops the warmth reading as a filter
- Act three (shots 8–10): return to the act-one warmth. The grade itself tells the story
- Skin protected throughout, orange-red vector unclipped, no smoothing
- Soft highlight rolloff, light halation on speculars, fine 35mm grain ~2%
- **No teal-and-orange.** Nothing dates a launch film faster

## Sound

Sound does more work here than picture.

**Music.** Solo piano, felt-damped, close enough to hear the mechanism. It should sound
like someone practising in the next apartment, not like a soundtrack.
- 0:00–0:06 — mid-phrase, already playing, as if we arrived late to something happy
- 0:06 — **cut the music dead** on the hard cut to morning. Silence is the loudest
  thing in the film
- 0:08–0:16 — single notes return, sparse, unresolved
- 0:19 — the phrase from the opening comes back, and *now* we recognise it
- 0:27 — resolves, decays under the card

That returning phrase is the whole score. The audience won't consciously notice; they'll
feel the film close a loop.

**Ambience** (always present — silence is what makes AI footage feel synthetic): street
traffic at distance, a scooter, sparrows in ficus, café clatter and an espresso machine,
playground foley — chain swing, rubber matting, children at conversational distance,
never shrieking.

**Design.**
- Shot 7: **no UI sound at all.** Silence on the interface beat is more premium than any chime
- One soft haptic-like *tick* on the join tap — the only designed effect in the film,
  and therefore the brand's sonic signature
- Ambience crossfades into the playground **four frames before** the picture cut at 0:19.
  Sound arrives before image; the audience gets there first
- First human voice in the entire film is the laugh at 0:26

**Mix:** −16 LUFS, true peak −1.5 dBTP. Duck 3 dB under the card.

---

## Voiceover: no. And I want to be explicit about why.

I wrote three versions to be sure. Every one made the film smaller.

A voice explaining that these women don't know each other is a voice describing what
you can already see — and the instant someone narrates a feeling, the audience stops
having it. Worse, VO would force the film into the register it must avoid: the
explanatory, faintly pitying charity voice that would sink it in a municipal room.

The film speaks once, at the end, in type, in silence.

---

## The line

> ### הם היו כאן כל הזמן.
> **NestUp**

*They were here all along.*

Four words, and the preceding 27 seconds exist to load them. It doesn't describe the
product — it resolves the film. And to a municipality it says precisely the right thing:
**the community already exists in this city; it just can't find itself.** That positions
NestUp as missing infrastructure rather than an app to be bought.

**Alternate, parent-facing, warmer:** הקהילה שלכם קרובה יותר ממה שחשבתם.

Set in **Heebo** or **Assistant**, right-aligned, `#FAF8F4` ground. Never allow a Latin
font to substitute Hebrew glyphs. Both cards cost nothing — they're type composites.

## Generation order

1. **`ref_maya.png`, `ref_dana.png`.** Nothing else starts. Every face shot depends on

## The final question, answered

> *If the NestUp logo appeared only in the final two seconds, would this still be a great
> commercial?*

Yes — and that's why this is the concept. Strip every brand mark and Concept C is still a
film about living four metres from someone who would have been your friend. The product
never argues for itself; it just removes the obstacle, once, for three seconds, in the
middle.

The audience remembers the sleeping child on the wrong shoulder.
NestUp just gives that feeling a name.
